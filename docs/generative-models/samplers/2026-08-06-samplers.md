## Today's Question

How do samplers trade quality, speed, and likelihood? Day 1 focuses on the mathematical core: what lets a training-free sampler go from ~1000 steps to ~10–20 steps without retraining, and how is the remaining error distributed?

## Main Paper

### Metadata

- **Title:** DPM-Solver: A Fast ODE Solver for Diffusion Probabilistic Model Sampling in Around 10 Steps
- **Authors:** Cheng Lu, Yuhao Zhou, Fan Bao, Jianfei Chen, Chongxuan Li, Jun Zhu (Tsinghua)
- **Year:** 2022 (ICLR 2023)
- **Venue:** ICLR 2023 / arXiv:2206.00927
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00927

### Why this paper?

DPM-Solver is the canonical "fast sampling" paper — the one that made 10–20 step sampling practical and became the mathematical backbone of modern diffusion samplers. It answers today's question directly: it shows the probability-flow ODE has an exact analytic solution whose only numerically-hard part is an *exponentially weighted integral of the network output*, and that a high-order treatment of just that integral yields quality/speed trade-offs far beyond generic ODE solvers. It also connects naturally to what we already covered: DDIM (first-order, seen in diffusion-foundations) becomes a special case, and the consistency models seen in score-models attack the same NFE wall from the opposite direction.

### Core Problem

Diffusion sampling requires hundreds to thousands of sequential neural-network evaluations (NFE). Training-free samplers like DDIM are first-order discretizations of the diffusion ODE; too few steps → large discretization error → degraded FID. The question is whether the specific ODE structure can be exploited so that ~10–20 steps suffice, with **no retraining and no model changes**.

### Main Idea

Treat sampling as solving a *semilinear* ODE whose linear part can be integrated exactly:

1. The diffusion ODE (probability flow) is `dx/dt = f(t)x + (g(t)²/2)·(network term)` — the drift is linear in x, so the homogeneous part is solvable in closed form.
2. After a change of variable to log-SNR `λ = log(α_t/σ_t)`, the exact solution takes the form
   `x_t = (α_t/α_s)·x_s − α_t·∫_s^t e^{−λ} ε_θ(x_λ, λ) dλ`.
3. The **only** numerical approximation needed is the exponentially weighted integral of the network `ε_θ`. DPM-Solver designs high-order approximations of this integral — DPM-Solver-1/2/3 — with convergence-order guarantees.
4. Multistep variants reuse previous network evaluations to estimate the derivatives of `ε_θ`, so higher order does not cost extra function evaluations.

### Technical Details

- **Exact solution, not black-box solving:** prior samplers handed the whole ODE to generic solvers (Euler, RK); DPM-Solver integrates the linear part analytically and isolates the integral.
- **Change of variable:** `λ = log(α_t/σ_t)` (log signal-to-noise ratio) makes the integrand `e^{−λ} ε_θ` explicit — this is where the "exponential weighting" in the title comes from.
- **Order hierarchy:**
  - DPM-Solver-1: first-order; recovers DDIM-style updates in the continuous-time limit.
  - DPM-Solver-2: second-order; uses two previous ε evaluations (multistep) or one midpoint evaluation (single-step) to cancel first-order error.
  - DPM-Solver-3: third-order; three previous ε values; `O(h³)` convergence guarantee.
- **Generality:** works for both discrete-time and continuous-time DPMs; no retraining, no change to the trained network.
- **Results (CIFAR10, unconditional):** FID 4.70 @ 10 NFE, 2.87 @ 20 NFE; **4–16× speedup** vs. previous training-free samplers on various datasets.

### Research takeaway

The speed–quality trade-off in diffusion sampling is fundamentally a **numerical-analysis problem**. Once you see the semilinear structure of the probability-flow ODE, a "sampler" is just a quadrature rule for an exponentially weighted integral. All the gains come from high-order quadrature; the network itself never changes. This reframes "sampling quality" as "discretization error per function evaluation."

### Modern perspective

The DPM-Solver family became the production default: DPM-Solver++ (tomorrow's paper) extended it to classifier-free guided sampling, and `diffusers` uses DPM-Solver++ 2M by default for Stable Diffusion. The line continued to DPM-Solver-v3 (empirical model statistics computed from the pretrained model) and merged with distillation ideas (D-ODE solvers) for <10-step regimes. It also connects to consistency models (already covered): both attack the same NFE wall — better discretization vs. a direct learned few-step map.

## Related Papers

### Paper 1: Fast Sampling of Diffusion Models with Exponential Integrator (DEIS)

- **Authors:** Qinsheng Zhang, Yongxin Chen (Georgia Tech)
- **Year:** 2022
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13902

**Contribution:** An independent, roughly-concurrent approach to the same problem. Systematically analyzes the sampling procedure and identifies *discretization method* as the most crucial quality factor; leverages the semilinear structure of the probability-flow ODE with an **exponential integrator** that computes the linear part exactly, and approximates the remaining integral by high-order polynomial interpolation of the (reparameterized) score. SOTA at low NFE at the time: FID 4.17 @ 10 NFE, 3.37 @ 15 NFE (CIFAR10). Also introduces **score reparameterization** (multiplying the score by the noise std σ_t) to keep the integrand well-conditioned.

**Relation to main paper:** Same mathematical insight from two lenses — DPM-Solver via change-of-variable/exact solution; DEIS via the exponential-integrator toolbox of numerical analysis. The convergence of two independent derivations on the same semilinear/exponential structure is strong evidence this is the *right abstraction* for sampler design.

**Why it matters:** Provides the numerical-analysis vocabulary (exponential integrators, stiff vs non-stiff terms) that recurs throughout the topic, and the score-reparameterization idea later reappears in DPM-Solver++ and DEIS-SN.

**Deep read later:** Not necessary — DPM-Solver is the cleaner formal statement; DEIS is worth remembering as the alternative derivation and for its reparameterization analysis.

### Paper 2: UniPC — A Unified Predictor-Corrector Framework for Fast Sampling of Diffusion Models

- **Authors:** Wenliang Zhao, Lujia Bai, Yongming Rao, Jie Zhou, Jiwen Lu (Tsinghua)
- **Year:** 2023 (NeurIPS 2023)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04867

**Contribution:** Unifies predictor–corrector design for diffusion sampling. **UniP** is an arbitrary-order predictor derived from the exact-solution formulation; **UniC** is a corrector that can be appended to *any* existing sampler to raise its order of accuracy **without extra function evaluations**; UniPC = UniP + UniC with a unified analytical form for any order. Results: FID 3.87 (CIFAR10 unconditional) and 7.51 (ImageNet 256, conditional) @ 10 NFE, with the largest gains in the <10 NFE regime where DPM-Solver/DEIS degrade.

**Relation to main paper:** Direct follow-up in the same line. DPM-Solver showed how to build high-order *predictors*; UniPC shows a *corrector* can boost effective order "for free" by reusing past evaluations — the same predictor–corrector idea from Song's score-SDE work, transplanted into the fast-solver setting.

**Why it matters:** Predictor–corrector is a recurring, generalizable design pattern (it also appears in SDE/Langevin sampling and in later analyses of CFG as a predictor–corrector). UniC's "order boost without extra NFE" is the key mechanism for the extreme few-step regime and bridges toward the distillation/consistency line.

**Deep read later:** Maybe — the corrector derivation is subtle; a second pass after DPM-Solver++ would solidify the guided-sampling connection.

## Current Understanding

Samplers for diffusion are **quadrature rules for the probability-flow ODE**, and the entire design space is organized around one object: the exponentially weighted integral of the network output.

1. **The math core** — the diffusion ODE solution is analytic except for an integral; the log-SNR change of variable `λ = log(α_t/σ_t)` makes the weight `e^{−λ}` explicit; every serious training-free sampler approximates this integral.
2. **Order hierarchy** — DDIM (1st order, ~100 steps) → DPM-Solver / DEIS (2nd–3rd order, 10–20 steps) → UniPC (arbitrary order + corrector, <10 steps). Higher order cancels more discretization error per step but needs either stored past evaluations (multistep) or intermediate evaluations (single-step).
3. **The cost metric is FID-per-NFE** — quality is measured as empirical Pareto curves (FID vs. NFE), not by theory; sampler papers are judged by how far left/down the curve moves.
4. **Determinism** — ODE solvers are deterministic (same noise → same image); stochasticity is a separate dial (SDE solvers, noise injection) traded against diversity — likely tomorrow's follow-up angle.
5. **What is NOT solved** — guided sampling breaks high-order solvers (the DPM-Solver++ motivation); <5 NFE requires distillation or consistency-style ideas; likelihood (not FID) has different optimal operating points.

## Key Concepts

- **Diffusion ODE / probability-flow ODE** — the deterministic ODE whose solution is the sampling trajectory
- **Log-SNR `λ = log(α_t/σ_t)`** — the natural time coordinate for the weighted integral
- **Exponentially weighted integral** — `∫ e^{−λ} ε_θ dλ`; the only numerically-hard part of sampling
- **Semilinear structure** — linear drift integrated exactly; nonlinear network term approximated
- **High-order multistep solvers (DPM-Solver-1/2/3)** — reuse previous ε evaluations to cancel discretization error
- **Convergence order guarantee** — `O(h^order)` error in step size h
- **Exponential integrator (DEIS)** — numerical-analysis view: exact linear part + polynomial interpolation of the score
- **Score reparameterization** — multiplying score by σ_t (or dividing by its mean magnitude, DEIS-SN) keeps the integrand well-conditioned
- **Predictor–corrector (UniPC)** — UniP arbitrary-order predictor + UniC order-boosting corrector with no extra NFE
- **NFE (number of function evaluations)** — the cost metric; FID-per-NFE is the quality–speed Pareto

## Open Questions

- Why do high-order solvers become unstable under large classifier-free guidance scales — is the guided ODE numerically stiff, and can it be fixed without thresholding heuristics?
- Is there a principled (not empirical) theory of the optimal order-vs-NFE trade-off for a given model and noise schedule?
- Does solver choice matter *more* for small/undertrained models (stiffness near the end of the trajectory)? Recent flow-matching benchmarks hint that the low-order vs high-order gap widens for imperfect models.
- Can corrector-based order boosting (UniC) be made fully adaptive — allocating step budget per trajectory from local error estimates?
- What is the right stochasticity schedule — when does injecting noise into a high-order ODE solver improve diversity without hurting FID (SA-Solver / DPM-Solver-v3 territory)?
- Do fast ODE solvers change the likelihood–quality gap — is the best-FID operating point also the best-likelihood point?

## Possible Thesis Ideas

- **Adaptive-order exponential-integral solvers** — estimate local discretization error of the weighted integral and pick order/step size per step (analogous to adaptive RK), specialized to the `e^{−λ}` weight
- **Guidance-stable high-order solvers** — design solver updates whose stability is provably independent of guidance scale, replacing thresholding hacks
- **Correctness theory for predictor–corrector samplers** — convergence guarantees for UniC-style correctors under the exponential-integral formulation
- **Solver-aware distillation** — distill few-step students that match a specific high-order solver trajectory (bridging D-ODE solvers and consistency distillation; connects to the consistency-models topic)
- **Per-trajectory NFE allocation** — a lightweight controller that predicts which trajectories are easy/hard and allocates solver budget accordingly
